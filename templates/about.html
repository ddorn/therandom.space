About this website

I'm Diego Dorn. I find it fascinating to see how order and meaning can emerge from a sequence of random bytes, and I've used randomness in many projects, from random images to random fractals.

In 2021, recovering from an appendicitis and a bit bored, I improved an old random word generator to make Alsatian town names, proverbs and film titles. We had a hell of a good time with friends and family, so I wanted to share them ;)

How does it work?

All the generators work the same way:

  • I gather a large amount of the things I want to generate: a corpus.
  • From the corpus, I compute the probability that a word follows a given sequence of words.
  • I pick the start of a sentence from the corpus and complete it, word by word, according to those probabilities.

The sequences of N consecutive words are the N-grams of the corpus, and the important parameter is N. New proverbs appear when two proverbs share N-1 words but continue differently. With a large N, sentences are well formed but this rarely happens, so almost every proverb already exists. With a small N, everything is new... but nonsensical:

> Mieux vaut ne juge pas une chose bien pour faire naître ? ne me faire dire, le long sérieux.

That's N = 2. Proverbs here use N = 3: not perfect, but it works often enough to be fun! The source code is on GitHub.
